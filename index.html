What am I looking at?

A kernel is a 3 × 3 grid of nine learned weights, connecting one input channel to one output channel. Many kernels make up a convolution; two convolutions make up each block in this study.

How to explore

Use Next with a match to skip kernels with no partners. Red marks your selected kernel and blue marks its partners. Change the comparison or cutoff to ask a different similarity question.

The main finding

Similar kernels do not guarantee reusable blocks.

Useful block reuse was rare without retraining. The explorer shows structural similarity between individual kernels. The separate replacement and bypass experiments test what happens to the network's predictions when a whole block is changed. Their results are in the report.

Choose a network snapshot

Trained is the checkpoint with the highest validation accuracy. Initial is that same network's random starting point, before learning. A seed identifies an independent training run. Kernel IDs are local to each snapshot.

A few useful distinctions

What counts as a matching partner?

A partner is another kernel whose saved distance from the selected kernel is at or below your chosen cutoff. A smaller cutoff is stricter. Distances are not similarity percentages, and self-pairs are excluded.

Why are there three comparisons?

Signed shape compares patterns after removing magnitude. Original weight values retains magnitude differences. Centered shape also removes each kernel's average value. Together they help distinguish similar patterns from similar average levels or strengths.

Can I download the numbers?

Yes. Every explorer provides full-precision CSV preview, copy and download for the selected kernel's partners, including all nine weights. These controls read saved results. They do not train models or change any weights.
